about fathers and sons usually about the sons being inadvertently destroyed by the fathers what was the genesis of this book how is your relationship with your father and did the stories all come one after another or did the theme emerge only slowly the theme was there from the start but the form took a while to find first i tried to write a more conventional novel about fathers and sons i would start with a father and a son arranged in some extremely ironic configuration as in these stories but then i would make the son sort of amble over to the father in a realist mode and say something and the novel would fall apart the next morning i would start from scratch with a slightly different extremely ironic configuration and one character would go over to the other and say something and the novel would fall apart this went on for several years during which i felt and probably seemed increasingly crazy finally i realized that instead of representing the psychology of the relationship directly realistically which i was evidently bad at and didn t enjoy i could let it emerge indirectly from the juxtaposition of dozens of these ironic configurations each standing on its own and spun out according to its own logic i have a great relationship with my father dysfunctional relationships are less interesting to me than the seam of dysfunction that runs through every relationship rendering communication impossible imprisoning us in our own heads etc there s something appealingly mathematical about these stories you can see them as variations or iterations on a theme in a way there s something formulaic about them in a good way were there models you drew on works that are more experimental than much mainstream fiction did you have a phase of writing more realistic work before ditching it yes many models i m drawn to fiction that seems to consist of some obscure logic working itself out rather than a series of plot points so kafka but also the deranged conceptual analysis of thomas bernhard the rigorous whimsy of daniil kharms the absurd inquiries of gert hofmann borges beckett calvino and kleist more recently lydia davis and donald antrim i also had in mind the repetitions and permutations of sketch comedy philosophy and children s books one of the qualities that makes the stories so good to my mind is that they combine that commitment to working out a formula with a real sense of humor and pleasure in the sound of the writing how careful were you to ensure that the stories weren t just exercises in my favorite fiction i get the feeling that the successful working out of the book s logic is somehow important for the continued sanity of its author for me calvino and borges as great as they are are sometimes too placid and controlled too sane to match the best of kafka and bernhard i guess like some outdated romantic i want my writers constantly teetering on the edge of a breakdown anyway among other shortcomings i am definitely too sane so when a story went wrong as it often did it usually resulted in a way too controlled illustration or parody of some thought or idea which is what i think of as an exercise those are the stories i tried to cut from the book though i m sure some remain do you feel done with patrimony what would you like to tackle next you know those comedy bits that involve repeating the same gag until it book how is your relationship with your father and did the stories all come one after another or did the theme emerge only slowly the theme was there from the start but the form took a while to find first i tried to write a more conventional novel about fathers and sons i would start with a father and a son arranged in some extremely ironic configuration as in these stories but then i would make the son sort of amble over to the father in a realist mode and say something and the novel would fall apart the next morning i would start from scratch with a slightly different extremely ironic configuration and one character would go over to the other and say something and the novel would fall apart this went on for several years during which i felt and probably seemed increasingly crazy finally i realized that instead of representing the psychology of the relationship directly realistically which i was evidently bad at and didn t enjoy i could let it emerge indirectly from the juxtaposition of dozens of these ironic configurations each standing on its own and spun out according to its own logic i have a great relationship with my father dysfunctional relationships are less interesting to me than the seam of dysfunction that runs through every relationship rendering communication impossible imprisoning us in our own heads etc there s something appealingly mathematical about these stories you can see them as variations or iterations on a theme in a way there s something formulaic about them in a good way were there models you drew on works that are more experimental than much mainstream fiction did you have a phase of writing more realistic work before ditching it yes many models i m drawn to fiction that seems to consist of some obscure logic working itself out rather than a series of plot points so kafka but also the deranged conceptual analysis of thomas bernhard the rigorous whimsy of daniil kharms the absurd inquiries of gert hofmann borges beckett calvino and kleist more recently lydia davis and donald antrim i also had in mind the repetitions and permutations of sketch comedy philosophy and children s books one of the qualities that makes the stories so good to my mind is that they combine that commitment to working out a formula with a real sense of humor and pleasure in the sound of the writing how careful were you to ensure that the stories weren t just exercises in my favorite fiction i get the feeling that the successful working out of the book s logic is somehow important for the continued sanity of its author for me calvino and borges as great as they are are sometimes too placid and controlled too sane to match the best of kafka and bernhard i guess like some outdated romantic i want my writers constantly teetering on the edge of a breakdown anyway among other shortcomings i am definitely too sane so when a story went wrong as it often did it usually resulted in a way too controlled illustration or parody of some thought or idea which is what i think of as an exercise those are the stories i tried to cut from the book though i m sure some remain do you feel done with patrimony what would you like to tackle next you know those comedy bits that involve repeating the same gag until it becomes tedious and then pushing through until it becomes funny again i m hoping that a writing career can be something like that which is to say i m not sure i m done with patrimony just yet but i am looking for a new form your story in this week s issue the philosophers makes up part of a larger work called inherited
